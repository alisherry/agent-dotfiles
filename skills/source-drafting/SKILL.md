# Source Drafting

Read the supplied sources before drafting. Treat explicit user instructions as authoritative and do not silently replace their requested structure or audience.

1. Build a compact claim map: the intended claim, its supporting source, and any uncertainty.
2. Separate sourced facts from interpretation. Label an inference when it matters to the reader's decision.
3. Draft for the requested audience and medium. Prefer plain language and retain meaningful qualifications.
4. Cite claims near the text they support. Never invent a source, quotation, statistic, or date.
5. Verify the final draft against the claim map. Remove unsupported specificity and flag material gaps.

When the sources conflict, describe the conflict and use the more authoritative or current source only when that choice is justified.
